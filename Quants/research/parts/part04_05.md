## PART 4 — THE BIGGEST UNSOLVED PROBLEM: A GENUINELY COUPLED MULTI-OBJECTIVE SYSTEM

### 4.1 The interaction map (where optimizing one thing hurts another)

| If you push this… | …this gets worse | Mechanism (and where the evidence comes from) |
|---|---|---|
| **Max GPU utilization / power** | Thermal margin, failure rate, grid peaks | More sustained power → higher coolant/chip temperatures → throttling and higher hazard (Meta: 700 W parts at limits); simultaneous full-power jobs create synchronized swings (Microsoft/OpenAI/NVIDIA). |
| **Cut site power quickly (DR event)** | Training goodput, inference SLOs, checkpoint storms | Uniform capping slows all jobs equally, including memory-bound phases where it saves little power (Zeus/Perseus); suspending jobs forces checkpoints (I/O burst); scaling inference down risks p99 TTFT. |
| **Lower GPU clocks (DVFS) for energy** | Latency, iteration time — but *non-uniformly* | Compute-bound prefill/forward ops slow ~linearly; memory-bound decode/all-reduce phases barely slow → the trade-off curve is concave and job-specific (Perseus frontier). |
| **Raise coolant supply temperature (save chiller energy, water)** | Thermal headroom, GPU leakage power, reliability | Warm-water cooling (45 °C inlet, NVIDIA DSX) saves energy but reduces transient headroom during a CDU fault and raises static leakage; hot GPUs fail more. |
| **Consolidate inference onto fewer replicas at night** | Tail latency, blast radius, restart time | Fewer, fuller replicas → higher queueing delay; a failure removes a larger share; cold-starting a 70B+ model takes minutes. |
| **Migrate/resize jobs to shed power or chase prices** | Network contention, fragmentation, restart overhead | Elastic resizing changes all-reduce group sizes and creates spine congestion (Sia, Meta RoCE); migration needs checkpoint transfer. |
| **Chase real-time electricity prices** | Grid security, ramp violations, demand charges | Price-driven load following can *create* grid instability and ramp-limit violations (arXiv 2604.06924; AESO); a single 15-minute peak sets a month of demand charges. |
| **Use batteries to hide swings** | Capex, cycling degradation | 65 J/GPU capacitors handle sub-second; hours-long events need MWh batteries whose cycle life is consumed by every event. |
| **Protect reliability (fewer power-mode switches, no throttling of flaky nodes)** | Flexibility capacity, cost | Constrains the controller's cheapest moves. |

### 4.2 The unified problem statement

> **A GPU cluster must deliver a contracted, time-varying, priced amount of power flexibility to the grid (and exploit price/carbon variation) by choosing, every few minutes, the power level of every training job, the replica count and configuration of every inference service, the cooling supply-temperature setpoint, and battery dispatch — such that total cost (energy + demand charges + flexibility penalties + training delay cost + SLO-violation cost + reliability cost) is minimized, subject to a site power cap, ramp-rate limits, rack thermal limits, per-job deadlines, per-service latency SLOs, min-up/min-down times, switching limits, and network/placement feasibility, under uncertainty in inference demand, prices, weather and failures.**

This is:
- **multi-objective** (cost, goodput, SLO attainment, carbon, reliability — with a Pareto frontier that operators must *see* to sign contracts);
- **multi-timescale** (day-ahead commitments → 5-minute re-planning → sub-second smoothing handled in hardware);
- **mixed discrete/continuous** (job power modes, replica counts, suspensions are integers; setpoints, battery power are continuous);
- **dynamic** (thermal state and battery state carry across time; work remaining evolves);
- **stochastic** (demand, prices, failures);
- **physically grounded** (every curve comes from measurements: Perseus/Zeus DVFS curves, POLCA/TAPAS inference power, NLR 0.1-s profiles, RC thermal models, chiller COP curves).

No published open system solves it jointly. Google shapes batch capacity day-ahead. Emerald/DSX Flex apply priority rules. nvPAX allocates caps fairly without a cost model. TAPAS handles inference thermal placement with static limits. Perseus optimizes one job. "Inference as Flexibility" handles ramps with batch size and a battery. The joint, cost-priced, forecast-driven, thermally-coupled controller — and an *open benchmark* to compare controllers — is the gap.

## PART 5 — RESEARCH-TO-PROBLEM MAPPING

| # | Research paper | Research insight | Infrastructure problem | Current industry approach | Research gap | Proposed innovation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Perseus (SOSP'24)** | A training job has a concave time–energy frontier; 10–30% of energy is "bloat" | P2, P21 — curtailment with unknown throughput cost | Uniform power caps; pause/kill | Frontier used per job, never for cluster-level allocation under time-varying caps | **Frontier-based power allocation:** shed bloat first (free), then trade along frontiers by $/goodput |
| 2 | **Pollux (OSDI'21)** | Goodput = throughput × statistical efficiency; allocation must be co-adaptive | P4, P23 — utilization & fairness; P2 | Static GPU counts | Goodput never combined with power as an allocation dimension | **Goodput-per-watt allocation** with elastic GPU count *and* power level |
| 3 | **TAPAS (ASPLOS'25)** | Inference phases and configs have distinct thermal/power profiles; headroom is schedulable | P7, P10, P11 | Rack caps; reactive throttling | Static limits; inference-only; air-cooled | Include inference power/thermal curves in a **shared envelope with training** and liquid-cooled thermal dynamics |
| 4 | **Google Carbon-Aware Computing (TPS'23)** | Hourly capacity curves (VCCs) are a workable grid↔scheduler interface | P2, P20 | Day-ahead VCCs for batch | Hourly, opaque, no goodput cost, no events | **5-minute VCC-like envelopes** computed by MPC with explicit cost curves and event response |
| 5 | **Meta Reliability (HPCA'25)** | MTTF ∝ 1/#GPUs; lemons; ETTR is the metric | P6, P22 | Fixed checkpoint intervals; spares | Reliability decoupled from power/thermal actions | **Reliability constraints in the controller:** checkpoint-before-suspend, switch limits, no throttling of suspect nodes |
| 6 | **Power Stabilization (MS/OpenAI/NVIDIA'25)** + GB300 | Training power oscillates at iteration frequency; hardware can smooth sub-second | P3 | Capacitors, power-burner mode | Coordination across jobs/inference at minutes-scale is open | Treat sub-second smoothing as a hardware constraint (ramp limits) inside the minutes-scale MPC |
| 7 | **Emerald Phoenix demo (Nature Energy)** | 25%/3 h flexibility is achievable with QoS preserved | P2 | Rule-based Conductor | No optimality, no published trade-off frontier, proprietary | **Open benchmark + optimal controller** that reports the Pareto frontier of flexibility vs cost |
| 8 | **Lazic et al. MPC cooling (NeurIPS'18)** | Linear/learned thermal models + MPC work in production | P7, P8 | Vendor BMS control | Cooling MPC never sees the workload plan | **Workload-informed cooling setpoints** solved jointly with power caps |
| 9 | **DynamoLLM / POLCA** | Inference power is near-linear in load; oversubscription is safe with phase awareness | P10, P21 | Autoscalers on latency only | Energy/power not in the autoscaling objective | **SLO-constrained replica scaling as a flexibility lever** with dollar-valued SLO risk |
| 10 | **Inference as Flexibility (2606.21833)** | Batch-size modulation can offset training ramps and cut BESS use 71% | P3, P19 | BESS-only | Only ramp management; no cost, no thermal, no training levers | Fold into the fast layer; combine with training DVFS and BESS in one program |
| 11 | **nvPAX (2605.01837)** | Hierarchical power allocation is a fast QP/LP | P21 | Fair share | No value, SLO, thermal or grid context | Replace fairness objective with **cost-weighted goodput/SLO objective** and add dynamics |
| 12 | **Hu et al. Acme (NSDI'24) + Azure traces** | Public traces of LLM dev jobs, failures, and inference demand exist | All | Proprietary evaluations | No open, trace-driven benchmark for power-flexible control | **HeadroomBench:** open digital twin + baselines + metrics |

**Strongest intersections (selected):** rows 1, 3, 4, 7, 9 and 12 — they combine into one controller (rows 1+3+9 are the levers, 4 is the interface, 7 is the evidence that it matters and the baseline to beat, 12 is the evaluation vehicle). Rows 5, 6, 8 become constraints/sub-models rather than separate projects.
